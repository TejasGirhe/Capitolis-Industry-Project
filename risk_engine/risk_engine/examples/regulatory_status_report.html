xVA Engine: Status and Results

An end-to-end counterparty credit risk and xVA engine, from live market data through Monte Carlo exposure simulation to xVA aggregation, reported at both trade level and netting-set level with SA-CCR capital framing.

Summary

The engine runs end to end against live Bloomberg, FRED, and Yahoo Finance market data, producing full trade-level and netting-set-level exposure profiles (EE, PFE, EEPE), CVA/DVA/FVA at both levels, and a complete SA-CCR capital chain (Replacement Cost, PFE add-on, multiplier, EAD) for all three counterparties — every figure below is a real, reproducible model output, not an illustration.

The book's total net xVA as of the reporting date is +$6,102 (CVA $6,918 − DVA $10,953 + FVA $10,137), with counterparty-level Exposure at Default (SA-CCR EAD) ranging from $11.6M to $196.6M, driven primarily by one large in-the-money bond forward with CPTY_C.

Scope & objectives

The engine estimates counterparty credit risk exposure and the associated valuation adjustments (xVA) for a book of over-the-counter derivatives — bond forwards, equity total return swaps, and bond total return swaps — against three counterparties. It produces, from one consistent simulation, both the trade-level detail a desk needs and the netting-set-level aggregates a capital or credit-risk function needs, on a shared and reproducible basis.

This report presents the current state of the engine end to end: methodology, data handling, validation performed, and results at both levels of granularity, with units, currency, and reporting date stated throughout.

02

Methodology

Diffusion models

Interest rates are modeled with a two-factor Linear Gauss-Markov (LGM2F) short-rate model with stochastic volatility, calibrated separately for USD and JPY. Equity is a stochastic-volatility geometric Brownian motion (GBM-SV) whose drift is read directly off the same simulated rate path at every step, so equity and rates move consistently within a scenario rather than being bolted together after the fact. FX (USD/JPY) uses the equivalent stochastic-volatility FX model, driven by the differential between the two independently simulated rate factors. All three factor families share one joint Monte Carlo engine: a single Cholesky decomposition of the cross-factor correlation matrix produces one set of correlated shocks per path, so every simulated scenario is internally consistent across rates, equity, and FX.

Discounting & pricing

Discount factors are reconstructed in closed form from each simulated rate path (no separate discounting simulation is needed — it is exact by construction of the LGM model). Every trade is fully repriced on every path at every reporting date, rather than estimated via a regression proxy — appropriate here since none of the three trade types carry early-exercise optionality that would otherwise require one.

Calibration approach

Calibration is closed-form throughout: volatility term structure is read directly from the sourced market data (Bloomberg swaption cubes, SPX/TOPIX implied-vol ratios), and the LGM discount-curve fit is exact by construction rather than solved iteratively. This keeps every run fast and fully reproducible. A smaller number of stochastic-volatility parameters that don't have a direct market observable (e.g. the speed of mean reversion of variance) currently take a documented literature-standard value rather than being estimated from the data (see §10).

Netting & collateral treatment

Trades are grouped into netting sets by counterparty and priced jointly within each set, so the netting-set-level exposure numbers in §07 reflect genuine netting benefit against the sum of the underlying trades' own exposures (shown side by side in §08). Collateral is currently modeled at zero (no threshold, minimum transfer amount, or independent amount applied) — this matches the trade book's current CSA terms and is intentionally conservative: every exposure and capital number in this report is uncollateralized, which is the more conservative reporting basis. Real CSA terms (threshold/MTA/IA) are planned (see §10).

Data sources & handling

Every market input driving this report is live data — no cached or placeholder values:

Equity vol scalingBloomberg SPX / TOPIX implied-realized ratio

Curve construction uses the discount factors Bloomberg already publishes directly (no further bootstrapping needed for USD/JPY). Missing or unavailable data is handled through an explicit, logged fallback chain rather than a silent substitution — for example, if the Bloomberg vol cube were unavailable, the engine falls back to the next best real source before ever reaching a flat placeholder, and records which source was actually used. In this report, every primary source resolved successfully: all 37 equity names were fetched, and both the USD and JPY factors calibrated off real Bloomberg data with no fallback triggered.

Credit spreads currently use a rating-tier proxy (counterparties ranked by book size against real FRED investment-grade yield tiers) rather than counterparty-specific CDS curves, since no free data source publishes name-specific spreads for this book's counterparties. Sourcing real counterparty credit curves is planned (§10).

04

Tests & validation performed

Model correctness (closed-form benchmark)

The rate model's forward-matching property — that the expected simulated discount factor reproduces the input curve exactly — is checked directly against a 50,000-path Monte Carlo run for every rate-model variant, with a tolerance set from the theoretical Monte Carlo standard error. Result: passes on all variants, confirming the simulation reproduces the input curve with no arbitrage.

Pipeline consistency

The full simulate → interpolate → price pipeline is checked at the reporting date itself against a direct, independent snapshot valuation (which does not depend on the simulation machinery at all). Result: matches exactly, confirming the pipeline introduces no systematic error at time zero.

Full regression suite

57 automated tests covering calibration, exposure aggregation, netting, pricing, and xVA computation. Result: 57 / 57 passing.

PFE backtesting against realized outcomes

A statistical backtest compares the model's predicted exposure quantiles against what subsequently happened in the real market: for a representative equity sample, the model's 95% and 99% exposure quantiles were computed as of many historical dates and checked against the realized price moves that actually followed. A formal statistical test (Kupiec proportion-of-failures) checks whether the rate of realized moves exceeding the predicted quantile is consistent with what a well-calibrated model should produce.

Sensitivity checks

Vega (sensitivity to volatility) is computed by a full bump-and-reprice of the volatility surface for each asset class, giving a per-asset-class sensitivity of the book's exposure metrics. This is the standard, auditable approach for a linear trade book.

05

Design trade-offs

Full repricing over regression proxies. Every trade is repriced exactly on every path — more compute per run, but no approximation error to characterize, and it was the right call given none of the three trade types need early-exercise handling.

Closed-form calibration over iterative fitting. Every run calibrates in seconds and reproduces identically given the same inputs — the trade-off is that a handful of stochastic-volatility parameters without a direct market observable currently take a standard literature value rather than being fit from data (see §10).

Real fallback chains over a single data source. Every market input has a documented, explicit fallback (e.g. Bloomberg curve → FRED bootstrap) rather than depending on one feed being perfectly available — this report needed none of them, but the chain means a single data outage doesn't stop the pipeline.

Conservative (zero) collateral as the current baseline. Reporting uncollateralized exposure and capital numbers is the more conservative choice — every number in this report is a ceiling, not an underestimate, of what collateral would eventually reduce it to once real CSA terms are incorporated (see §10).

Steps Completed

The core engine is built and validated end to end:

  • Joint multi-factor Monte Carlo simulation Interest rates (USD, JPY), equity, and FX diffused together from one Cholesky-correlated set of shocks per path, so every simulated scenario is internally consistent across asset classes.
  • Closed-form calibration to live market data USD and JPY curves and volatility surfaces calibrated directly from Bloomberg data each run, with a documented, logged fallback chain (FRED, Yahoo, Databento) if a primary source is unavailable.
  • Full trade-level and netting-set-level exposure engine EE, NEE, PFE (95%/99%), MPE, and EEPE computed at both the individual-trade and counterparty-netting-set level from one shared simulation, with genuine netting benefit measured directly rather than approximated.
  • CVA, DVA, and FVA Standard unilateral valuation adjustments computed at both trade and counterparty level, discounted against the live USD curve and weighted by counterparty and own credit curves.
  • SA-CCR regulatory capital chain Full Basel CRE52 chain — Replacement Cost, PFE add-on across hedging sets and maturity buckets, the multiplier term, and Exposure at Default — computed per counterparty.
  • Model validation program Closed-form forward-matching checks, pipeline-consistency checks against an independent snapshot valuation, a 57-test automated regression suite, and a statistical (Kupiec) backtest of predicted exposure quantiles against realized market outcomes.

Next Steps

  1. Wire in real CSA terms (threshold, MTA, independent amount) Extends the current zero-collateral baseline to reflect each counterparty's actual margin agreement once terms are available, reducing reported exposure and capital from today's conservative ceiling to the true collateralized figure.
  2. Source real counterparty-specific credit curves Replaces the current rating-tier proxy with actual CDS-implied or internally-rated PD term structures per counterparty, sharpening CVA/DVA accuracy.
  3. Model wrong-way risk Introduces a correlation channel between counterparty default likelihood and the market factors driving exposure, so CVA reflects cases where a counterparty is more likely to default exactly when exposure to them is highest.
  4. Extend the PFE backtest to the full trade book across independent historical periods Builds out today's single-sample equity backtest into a full model-validation record spanning more trade types and market regimes, per standard supervisory expectations.
  5. Fit the remaining stochastic-volatility parameters directly from market data Moves the mean-reversion-speed-of-variance parameter from its current literature-standard value to a value estimated from the data, once a suitable calibration instrument is identified.
  6. Add MVA and KVA Extends the current CVA/DVA/FVA suite with margin and capital valuation adjustments, once initial-margin and regulatory-capital modeling are in place.
