Model Validation Memo — Internal Review Draft

How close is this to a regulatory-grade risk engine?

A ground-truth audit of the Capitolis counterparty credit risk / XVA engine, read at the level of individual formulas, calibration routines, and fallback chains — not the architecture diagram.

Scope risk_engine/ (models, calibration, simulation, pricing, netting, exposure, XVA, greeks, data, tests) Method direct source reading, file:line citations Trades covered Bond Forward · Equity TRS · Bond TRS

Bottom line

This is a well-built research and pricing-validation engine — not yet a regulatory-submission engine. The market-risk core (curve/vol calibration, joint Monte Carlo, exposure profiles) is unusually rigorous for a project this size, with real data, honest fallback disclosure, and genuine closed-form correctness tests. But three areas a regulator would test first — collateral/CSA modeling, wrong-way risk, and SA-CCR's capital chain — are either absent or stubbed out, and the credit side (PD, CVA) runs on a proxy, not real counterparty curves.

Put differently: if a model-validation team's first question is "does the market-risk engine behave correctly," the answer is largely yes, and it's tested. If their first question is "is the capital number right," the answer is no — that machinery doesn't exist yet.

01

Rate, equity & FX models

Partial

Short-rate model — LGM, 1F and 2F Present

A proper Linear Gauss-Markov (separable Hull-White) implementation, in all four registered variants: LGM1F, LGM1F_SV, LGM2F, LGM2F_SV. The discount-factor reconstruction is exact by construction:

DF(t,T) = DF(0,T)/DF(0,t) · exp( -Σᵢ Hᵢ(u)·xᵢ(t) - ½·Σᵢⱼ Hᵢ Hⱼ ρᵢⱼ √(ζᵢζⱼ) )
H(u) = (1 - e^(-a·u)) / a

models/lgm.py:16–20, 60–86 — verified against a martingale property test, see §10.

The 2F variance split between short- and long-rate factors is a hardcoded 70/30 split (frac2 = 0.30), described in-code as a "literature-typical prior," not something fit to the data.

Stochastic vol — a real gap between what's fit and what's simulated Partial

Volatility is stochastic via a CIR multiplier on the deterministic term-structure vol:

dv = κ(1 - v)dt + η√v·dW_v          (mean-reverts to 1, dimensionless)
σ_eff(t) = σ(t) · √v(t)

The correlation parameter ρ between the rate/asset shock and its own vol-of-vol shock is calibrated, reported in the model's assumptions — and then never applied. simulate_cir_variance_step takes (v_prev, κ, η, dt, z) with no leverage term tying z to the asset's own Brownian draw. The vol-of-vol shock is drawn fully independently for every factor (LGM, GBM, FXGBM alike). This means the engine reports a fit skew/leverage parameter that is inert in the actual paths — a real correctness gap, not just a modeling simplification.

models/lgm.py:157; models/_shared.py:106–112; same pattern in gbm.py:98–101 and fx.py.

Equity — GBM / GBM_SV Present

Drift is genuinely path-consistent — r(t) is read off the same simulated LGM path via discount_factor(), not a static curve. The same SV-correlation gap above applies here.

FX — FXGBM / FXGBM_SV, with a real dual-currency upgrade Present

Two modes exist: a preferred path where the foreign (JPY) rate is a genuinely simulated, jointly-correlated LGM factor calibrated off real Bloomberg JPY data; and a deterministic covered-interest-parity fallback when that data is unavailable. Both are coded, tested, and the choice between them is printed explicitly at runtime — never silent.

02

Calibration method

Partial

No iterative optimizer anywhere in the calibration path — everything is closed-form or a direct read off market data. That's a defensible design choice for the parts that are truly closed-form (LGM's forward-matching is exact by construction), but it also means several parameters that sound "calibrated" are really just literature defaults:

  • Volatility term structure — read directly off ATM pillars, not bootstrapped against option prices.
  • κ (mean-reversion speed of variance) is always the literature prior, for every asset class, including where a real vol cube exists — there's no swaption/cap grid in this data that identifies it.
  • FX smile/skew is never actually calibrated. The only FX vol data available is a single ATM snapshot, so the skew-fit routine always fails its own len(strikes) < 3 check and silently falls back to the hardcoded FX prior (κ=1.0, η=0.35, ρ=0.0).
  • The Bloomberg rate "smile" fit is along the swap-tenor axis, not moneyness — a labeling nuance that's documented in-code but easy to misread as real skew calibration.
03

Joint simulation & interpolation

Present

This is the strongest part of the codebase. A real N-factor joint Monte Carlo: one Cholesky decomposition of a cross-factor correlation matrix, sourced from historical realized correlation, with an unconditional PSD repair step (eigenvalue clipping to a strict positive floor) applied before every factorization — a defensive choice explicitly justified because LAPACK's Cholesky can fail on eigenvalues as small as -1.85e-15 that pass a naive tolerance check.

One documented simplification: a 2F rate model's two internal factors share a single cross-correlation to any other factor — there's no way to give the short- and long-rate components independently different correlations to, say, equity.

Brownian-bridge interpolation — correct, but the safer mode isn't the default Partial

A full conditional-variance bridge is implemented and mathematically correct, but production pricing runs use the mean-only bridge by default (add_bridge_noise=False). That understates variance between simulation-grid nodes for any exposure metric read off an off-grid date — which, given a 28-date simulation grid against ~94 regression dates, is most of them.

04

Pricing engine

Present

Full repricing per path per date — not regression-based. There is no Longstaff-Schwartz or any continuation-value estimator anywhere in the codebase, which is fine because there's nothing that needs it: exactly three trade types exist, all linear, European, with no early-exercise optionality — Bond Forward, Equity TRS, Bond TRS. No swaptions, no options of any kind are actually priced (despite swaption vol data being sourced for rate calibration).

A documented, deliberate bug-fix worth noting: the underlying pricer library doesn't check a trade's maturity against the valuation date, so this engine explicitly excludes matured trades per regression date — otherwise settled trades would keep contributing phantom exposure.

05

Netting

Partial

Netting sets are, by the code's own admission, a 1:1 stand-in for counterparties — trades are grouped purely by a .counterparty string. There's no representation of an actual ISDA Master Agreement, no support for multiple netting agreements per counterparty, and no cross-product netting-eligibility logic. The code is honest about this being deliberately deferred, not an oversight: the class is kept separate specifically so a real multi-agreement structure is "a config change later, not a redesign."

06

Exposure & collateral

Partial

Exposure metrics Present

The full standard set, at both 95% and 99%: EE, NEE, PFE, MPE, EEPE (Basel trapezoidal time-weighting over the first year), plus a non-standard conditional tail-mean extension beyond PFE. Exposure itself already bakes in a Margin Period of Risk — a 10-business-day close-out window with a 1-day variation-margin lag, both hardcoded defaults.

Collateral / CSA modeling Absent

There is exactly one margin model in the codebase, ZeroMargin, and it always returns zero collateral. No threshold, no minimum transfer amount, no independent amount exist anywhere in the tree. The module's own docstring lists these as explicitly out of scope, tied to a stated project assumption of no initial margin. MPoR is modeled as a fixed window, not as a property of an actual CSA.

This is the single largest gap standing between "exposure profile" and "regulatory exposure profile" — collateralization is usually the first thing a model-risk reviewer asks about.

07

Credit & XVA

Gaps

CVA / DVA / FVA Present

Standard unilateral formulas, correctly discounted and summed over the exposure profile's own anchor dates:

CVA = (1 − R_cpty) · Σₜ DF(0,t) · EE(t)  · PD_cpty(t₋,t)
DVA = (1 − R_own)  · Σₜ DF(0,t) · |NEE(t)| · PD_own(t₋,t)
FVA =                Σₜ DF(0,t) · EE(t)  · s_funding · Δt(t₋,t)

Probability of default — a rating-bucket proxy, not real counterparty credit Partial

Real FRED data (Moody's Aaa/Baa corporate yields, less the Treasury curve) sets two anchor spreads, and each counterparty is ranked by book notional and linearly interpolated a spread between them — larger relationship assumed more creditworthy. This is explicitly flagged in-code as an assumption, not a credit assessment; there's no term structure to the spread, and the own-entity (Capitolis) credit curve for DVA reuses the same mid-tier proxy.

Wrong-way risk Absent

Exposure and default probability are computed on entirely separate, uncorrelated pipelines. There is no channel anywhere that would let a counterparty's default likelihood move with the very market factors driving their exposure — the single most consequential CVA modeling risk in practice, and it isn't represented at all.

KVA, MVA, real CDS curves Absent

No capital valuation adjustment, no margin valuation adjustment, and no CDS-curve bootstrap or CDS instrument pricing exist anywhere in the tree.

08

Greeks & SA-CCR

Partial

Vega — bump-and-reprice, one-sided Present

A full precache→price→profile pipeline is re-run under a shifted vol surface and differenced against base:

vega = (metric_shocked − metric_base) / bump_size

Explicitly a forward, not central, difference — the code says so directly. No AAD or adjoint differentiation exists anywhere in the project; this is the standard, if computationally heavier, bump-and-reprice approach.

SA-CCR — only the first stage of the chain Partial

Supervisory duration and the Basel III Table 2 supervisory factors are correctly implemented, and per-asset-class delta is aggregated correctly. But that's where it stops:

10

Testing & validation

Partial

Closed-form correctness tests Present

Genuinely rigorous, not smoke tests. The forward-matching test simulates 50,000 paths per model variant and checks the martingale property E[DF(t,T)] ≈ DF(0,T)/DF(0,t) against an MC-noise-scaled tolerance — a real no-arbitrage check, run across all four LGM variants. A separate end-to-end test confirms the full precache → bridge-interpolate → price pipeline reproduces a direct snapshot valuation at t=0.

Regulatory-standard backtesting Absent

No test or module anywhere back-tests simulated PFE/EE against subsequently realized market outcomes — the standard supervisory expectation (e.g. Kupiec-style coverage testing, traffic-light backtesting) for an exposure model. What exists validates internal consistency; nothing validates predictive accuracy against history.
